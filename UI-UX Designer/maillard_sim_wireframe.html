Design note 1 of 3 · parameter picker — the core question
Guided ingredient explorer for the precursors · slider + numeric pair for the conditions
  • Precursors: guided explorer, not raw checkboxes. A flat checkbox list of ~40 sugars and amino acids gives no reason to pick one over another. The left rail groups them (Reducing sugars · Sulfur amino acids · Branched-chain · Other), and each row carries a one-line why plus an evidence chip tied to the selected targets ("→ 2-methyl-3-furanthiol"). Adding a row is one click; the row then appears in the formulation list on the right with an amount field.
  • Amounts: molar concentration (mM) typed, never a slider. Precursor ratio is the single most important number a flavour chemist controls; they think in mM and in ratios, and 5.0 vs 5.4 mM is a real difference a slider thumb cannot express. Each row therefore gets a numeric mM input, and the builder shows the derived sugar : amino-acid molar ratio live, because that ratio is what they actually reason about.
  • Conditions: slider AND numeric, bound together. pH, temperature and time are bounded physical parameters with a literature-supported window, so the slider's job is to show that window (the green "typical" band under the track) while the number box preserves precision. Matrix is a small closed set → chips, not a dropdown.
  • Presets first. Most sessions start from a known system. Three dashed preset pills seed the whole form in one click; everything stays editable afterwards.
Recommendation: guided explorer + typed mM + slider/numeric conditions + presets

Design note · validation
Validate continuously, block only on hard errors

Warnings appear as the value changes, never as a modal after Run. Two severities only: a coral error (missing amino acid, pH outside the model's supported range) disables Run and offers a one-click fix; a neutral notice (unusual but legal combination, e.g. 180 °C for 90 min, or a sugar:AA ratio above 5:1) leaves Run enabled and simply says what it will cost in confidence. Try dragging pH to 9 or temperature to 200 to see both.

Design note 3 of 3 · progress & timeout
Name the phases, never fake the percentage, change the offer at 10 s and at 30 s
  • 1–3 s (the common case). Named phases — Validating → Setting up 16 reaction families → Running Monte-Carlo → Ranking compounds. Reading four concrete lines makes a 3 s wait feel instant and, more importantly, teaches the user what the model actually does. That is the "<3 s feel" the brief asks for — achieved by making the wait informative rather than by lying about the duration.
  • The percentage is derived from completed phases, not from a timer. It can pause on a slow phase. A bar that smoothly reaches 90% and stops is the single fastest way to lose trust in a scientific tool.
  • At ~10 s the offer changes: a calm "taking longer than usual" note plus a Finish in the background escape, so a long run never holds the researcher hostage.
  • At 30 s it is a timeout, said plainly, with the formulation preserved and three ranked recovery options (retry · retry cheaper with 500 draws and honestly wider whiskers · edit inputs). Second timeout → queue it and notify; don't ask for a third wait.
Recommendation: phase-derived progress · 10 s background escape · 30 s honest timeout with a cheaper retry

Design note 2 of 3 · results visualisation
One ranked bar chart on a log axis, with the uncertainty drawn on the bar — not a radar, not a table first
  • Ranked horizontal bars beat a table for the primary view: the question is "which compounds dominate, and by how much" — a length comparison, which the eye does in one pass and a column of numbers does not. The numbers stay, right-aligned on each row, so nothing is lost.
  • Log scale is mandatory. Yields span 0.1–1000 ppb. On a linear axis every meaningful sub-ppb sulfur compound collapses to a hairline next to hexanal. Label the axis as log so nobody misreads bar length as linear.
  • Whiskers on the bar, not a separate confidence column. The whole value of the Monte-Carlo run is the spread; putting the 5th–95th percentile directly on the bar makes "rank 5 and rank 6 overlap completely, so their order is meaningless" a visual fact instead of a footnote. Overlapping whiskers are the honest way to stop over-reading a ranking.
  • Off-notes are a separate coral callout, not just coral bars. "Will it taste wrong?" is a different decision from "will it taste meaty", so it gets its own card with its own verdict and its own action.
  • Reaction-family attribution is the "why", so it sits below the "what" — one stacked contribution strip answering which of the 16 families produced the profile. Show the top 5 and roll the rest into "other"; sixteen legend entries is noise.
  • Deliberately not built: a sensory radar/spider chart. It looks scientific, encodes ordering arbitrarily, and the axes here aren't commensurable.
Recommendation: log-scale ranked bars + on-bar whiskers · off-note card · family strip · table as export, not as the default view

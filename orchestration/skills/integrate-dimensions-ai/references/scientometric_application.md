# Dimensions Scientometric Research Program — application template

Apply at: https://dimensions.ai/scientometric-research-program

The application form asks for three things: research aims, intended use of the data, and institutional affiliation. Approval typically arrives in 3-7 business days. The template below covers a flavor-chemistry literature-mapping project; personalize the bracketed placeholders before submitting.

---

## Section 1 — Research aims (2-3 sentences)

We are building a structured knowledge graph of meat-flavor chemistry research — Maillard reaction products, lipid oxidation pathways, volatile odorants, sensory analytics methods, and plant-based meat analog chemistry — to identify cross-cutting research gaps and map the global community of researchers working in the field. The project is conducted at [INSTITUTION] under the supervision of [SUPERVISOR / PI] in collaboration with the Good Food Institute (GFI), and is non-commercial in nature. Outputs will be published as open scientific reports rather than productized.

## Section 2 — Intended use of Dimensions data (3-4 sentences)

We will use the Dimensions DSL API to (a) retrieve publication metadata for flavor-chemistry topics that supplement existing coverage from OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, PubMed, and CrossRef, (b) link publications to grants and funders to identify well-funded research labs and emerging funding priorities in alt-protein flavor research, and (c) surface patents and clinical trials adjacent to academic publications — visibility we cannot get from purely-academic sources. We will pull on the order of 500-2,000 publications per month during the active research phase, well within the standard rate limits. All data is stored in a private Postgres database for internal analysis; no Dimensions data will be redistributed, republished, or commercialized.

## What reviewers look for

Three things, in order of importance:

1. **Genuine non-commercial research aims.** Be specific about what scientific question you're answering. Vague aims ("explore the literature") read as data-harvesting and tend to get declined.
2. **Methodology.** Describe how the data will be used analytically, not just stored. The graph + gaps-analysis framing in Section 2 works well.
3. **Institutional backing.** Affiliation with a recognized research institution helps significantly. A supervisor's email validates the project. If you're independent, an affiliation with a non-profit (like GFI) substitutes well.

## Things to AVOID in the application

- Don't promise to publish a "platform" or "tool" — that signals commercial intent.
- Don't request access "for personal interest" — the program is specifically for research output.
- Don't underspecify volume. "A few thousand papers per month" is fine; "as many as possible" reads as scraping.
